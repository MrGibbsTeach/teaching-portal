# Mr Gibbs Teach: Commercial Strategy

Written 21 Oct 2026. Living document: update it when facts change. Statements marked "hypothesis" are untested and must be validated before money is spent on them.

## 1. Where we are

- Stage 0: internal pilot. Nothing is being sold.
- Pilot group: the owner's own Year 11 and 12 AIT classes (ATAR, General, Foundations), this year into early 2027. A Year 9 or 10 group may be added depending on availability.
- Parked for now: Year 7 and 8 Digital Technologies and everything else.
- Product: student-led online courses with interactive 3D animations, check questions on every lesson, aligned to WA SCSA.
- Long-term aim (owner's target, to be validated): about $200k AUD per year within two years, and a way out of full-time classroom reliance. At Atomi's price of about $470 per student per year that is roughly 430 paying students. At half that price it is roughly 850.
- Constraint: all content is made in the owner's own time with personally paid tools.

## 2. Phases and gates

| Phase | When | Goal | Gate to move on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Pilot | Now to early 2027 | Prove students learn from it and teachers (you) save time. Collect evidence. | Owner sets thresholds after the first term of data. Measure: lesson completion, check-question results, student feedback, assessment results, hours spent per week by the owner. |
| 1. Small paid test | 2027 | Find out if anyone outside the classroom pays. Test price. Candidates: WA Y11 and 12 AIT students and families, small schools without a specialist teacher. | A handful of paying customers who stay and say why. Clear answer on which segment pays. |
| 2. Y7 to Y10 Digital Technologies | After Phase 1 | Homeschool and home-education families, WA first. | Repeat purchase or renewal. |
| 3. Other curriculums | Later | Reuse the platform for other subjects and states. | Only after one curriculum earns. |

Do not build a standalone platform or start selling before the Phase 0 gate. Do not spend on marketing before a Phase 1 customer says yes.

## 3. Competitor benchmark: Atomi (verified 21 Oct 2026 from atomi.com.au and getatomi.com)

- Price (Australia): about $9 per week billed yearly (about $470 per student per year) or $14 per week billed monthly. Students and parents get up to 7 subjects, schools up to 13. Free trial, no card needed.
- Offer: short video lessons, interactive lessons, smart quizzes, revision and exam practice, progress tracking, AI recommendations and personalised AI feedback, usage insights for parents.
- Reach: sells to schools, students, parents and home educators. Claims over 400,000 students on its exam prep page and an average score improvement of 11.2% (their claim, not independently checked).
- Subjects: hundreds across Years 7 to 12, with a Digital Tech subject listed under Technologies. It has published WACE Year 12 updates. Not confirmed: whether it has a WA Applied Information Technology course. Check this before using any claim about AIT.
- Positioning: study support and revision aligned to what the school teaches.

We cannot beat Atomi on breadth, brand or budget. Trying to be "Atomi but cheaper" loses. The aim is to be clearly better in one niche.

## 4. Where we can be better than Atomi (hypotheses to validate in the pilot)

Competitor-by-competitor detail (Education Perfect, Mathspace, Reading Eggs, Khan Academy, Grok Academy, Code.org and others), how the advantages carry beyond Digital Technologies, and the feature backlog seed are in `claude/competitive-advantage.md`.

1. **Interactive, not just video.** Atomi is video-first. Our 3D animations let students operate the thing (flip switches, step a CPU, send packets). This is the clearest difference. Test: do students using animations score better on check questions than those who skip them?
2. **A full course, not just revision support.** Atomi supports what a school is teaching. We can be a complete, sequenced, student-led course that works with no teacher in the room. This suits home education and small or remote schools.
3. **Deep WA AIT fit by a working WA teacher.** AIT is practical and project-based (client briefs, solutions, portfolios), which video and quiz platforms handle poorly. We can build the project scaffolds, marking guides and exam practice around the real WA assessment style.
4. **Evidence.** Real results from real classes. Atomi's strongest claims are broad; ours can be specific ("AIT students in this course did X").
5. **Narrow and cheaper to buy.** A single-course price can sit below a seven-subject plan, which suits families who only need one subject.

Honest limits: the animations can be copied given time, so the lasting advantage is teacher-built depth, evidence and a growing library, not the animations alone. Y11 and 12 ATAR is exam-linked and competes most directly with Atomi, so the first paying segment may be somewhere else (see section 5).

## 5. Who might pay (hypotheses, rank after Phase 1)

| Segment | Why they might pay | Concern |
|---|---|---|
| Y11 and 12 AIT students and parents in WA | Practical course help and exam practice for a subject with few resources | Small market, competes with Atomi and school teachers |
| Small or remote schools without a specialist AIT or Digital Technologies teacher | A student-led course that runs without a teacher | Schools buy slowly and need privacy and curriculum evidence |
| Home-education families, Years 7 to 10 | A complete Digital Technologies course for a subject many parents cannot teach | Needs the parked Y7 to Y10 content |
| Other teachers | Ready-made lessons and animations | Lower willingness to pay |

## 6. Pricing options (decide after pilot data, do not price yet)

1. Single course per year. Simple, undercuts multi-subject plans. Tradeoff: low price per customer.
2. Year-level bundle (for example all of AIT Y11 or Y12). Higher price, clear value. Tradeoff: fewer buyers.
3. Free sample lesson plus paid course, with a separate school or teacher licence. Best for trust and for schools. Tradeoff: more to build.

Recommendation: start with option 3 in Phase 1, test two price points on a small number of customers, and benchmark against Atomi's $470 per year.

## 7. Build rules for the product

- Judge every build by: does it help students learn, is it reusable across courses, does it move toward a sellable product.
- Build the platform curriculum-agnostic. Content is organised as course, unit, lesson, and blocks (explanation, animation, check question), and each lesson maps to a standards list. A new curriculum then means new content and a new standards map, not new software.
- Every lesson works with no teacher present and carries its own check questions.
- Capture the data that proves the product works: lesson completion and check-question results per student. This is both pilot evidence and a selling point.
- Take proven learning features from the education reference sites (see the handover) and keep what makes us different: the interactive animations, WA alignment and check questions.

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Action |
|---|---|
| Employer and IP. The owner is a CEWA school employee. Content was made in his own time with his own tools, but selling it may touch employment, secondary work or IP policy. | Check the school and CEWA policies before selling. Seek proper advice. This is not legal advice. |
| Student data and privacy. Pilot students are minors. | Confirm what the school permits and what consent is needed before collecting or showing student data. Keep data collection to what the pilot needs. |
| Syllabus change. SCSA has published a Computer Science ATAR course for teaching in 2027. | Check whether the Applied Information Technology courses change and what that means for the content. |
| Atomi or a rival fills the AIT gap. | Verify what Atomi offers for WA AIT. Move fast on evidence and the course library. |
| Owner time and credit. One person, limited AI budget. | Follow the cost rules in the handover. Build only for the pilot. |
| Market too small. | Phase 1 tests who pays before more is built. |
