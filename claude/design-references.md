# Design Reference Sites: Instructions for AI

Use these sites as inspiration references whenever you design or write any customer-facing asset for this project. They are galleries of real examples. They are not templates to copy.

## Rules

1. **Look before you build.** Before producing a page, email, deck, ad, post, or share image, fetch the relevant site(s) below with your web tools and study 3 to 5 current examples. Do not work from memory.
2. **Borrow patterns, not designs.** Take the structure, hierarchy, and technique. Never reproduce another brand's layout, copy, logo, or imagery. Our output must be original.
3. **Match the example to our audience.** Many examples are B2B SaaS. Adapt to our actual customers and industry instead of imitating tech-startup styling.
4. **State what you used.** In your reply, name which site(s) and which patterns informed the work (one or two lines). If a site could not be reached or is paywalled, say so and continue with what you could see.
5. **Offer options.** When the choice is open (pricing layout, nav type, hero style), propose 2 or 3 patterns with a one-line tradeoff each, then recommend one.
6. **Respect access limits.** Some sites limit free access. Do not try to bypass paywalls or logins. Use what is publicly visible.

## Typical workflow

1. Identify the asset type and pick the matching site from the table.
2. Fetch the site and review several current examples.
3. Write a short pattern summary: what the best examples have in common.
4. Build our original version using those patterns and our own brand, copy, and content.
5. Report which patterns you applied and why.

## Keep in mind

- These sites show what looks good. They do not prove what converts. Where it matters, suggest testing.
- Examples are weighted toward tech and design-forward brands. Prioritise clarity and usability over trendy styling.
- If a site changes, moves, or goes offline, tell the owner and suggest a replacement rather than silently skipping it.
