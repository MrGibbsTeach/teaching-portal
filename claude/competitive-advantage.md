# Competitive Advantage: Where Mr Gibbs Teach Is Better

Initial review written 21 Oct 2026; current reference updates added 23 Oct 2026. Facts come from each site's public pages on the stated review date unless marked "unverified". This doc is the saved research: read it instead of re-fetching the sites. Refresh a row only when a decision depends on it and the facts are stale. Statements about us are hypotheses until the pilot proves them.

## 1. The short answer

We do not out-scale anyone, so we do not compete on breadth, brand or free content. We win by combining four things no single competitor combines for Western Australian students:

1. **Hands-on interactive learning.** Students operate the thing (flip switches, step a CPU, send packets), not just watch or tick quizzes. Most rivals are video, drill or quiz first.
2. **A complete, student-led course.** A sequenced path with check questions and project scaffolds that works with no teacher in the room. Most rivals are a library, a revision tool or a classroom tool that assumes a teacher.
3. **WA SCSA alignment by a working WA teacher.** Local depth, including practical project-based courses that quiz platforms handle poorly.
4. **Evidence from real classes.** Specific results from our own pilot, not broad claims.

Supporting edge for home education: parent-light delivery (answers behind reveals, marking guides, progress records) so a parent who is not a subject expert can run it.

## 2. Does this work beyond Digital Technologies?

Yes in principle, with a condition. The framework (interactive scenes, check questions, student-led sequencing, standards map) is reusable. Each new subject still needs a subject expert to author the content. Hypotheses to test after the first curriculum earns:

| Advantage | How it transfers |
|---|---|
| Interactive hands-on scenes | Science (circuits, cells, forces), maths (visual explorers), geography and humanities (maps, timelines), any subject with a system or process |
| Complete student-led course | Any subject a parent cannot teach, small or remote schools without a specialist |
| Local curriculum alignment | Other states and other curriculums via a new standards map |
| Evidence from real classes | Needs a teacher-author running each course in a real class first |

Do not try to beat specialist engines at their own game: adaptive maths drill, early-reading games, or free generic video. Pair with them or stay out.

## 3. Competitor by competitor

### Atomi (atomi.com.au)
- Facts: video-first. About $470 per student per year ($9 per week billed yearly, $14 monthly), up to 7 subjects for students and parents, 13 for schools. Videos, interactive lessons, quizzes, revision and exam practice, progress tracking, AI feedback and recommendations. Hundreds of subjects Y7 to 12, including a Digital Tech subject. Sells to schools, students, parents and home educators. Claims 400,000+ students on exam prep and an 11.2% average score improvement (their claim). Publishes WACE Year 12 updates. Not confirmed: a WA Applied Information Technology course.
- Strong at: breadth, brand, polish, AI feedback, school sales.
- Gaps: passive video and quizzes, support for school work rather than a full sequenced course, thin on practical project-based subjects.
- We do better: interactive scenes, complete course path, project scaffolds, WA AIT depth, single-course pricing.
- Adopt: short lessons paired with a quiz, revision and exam practice mode, instant feedback, progress view.
- Do not fight on: breadth, brand, video production volume.
- **Student overview reference checked 23 Oct 2026:** Atomi's student Insights view counts completed videos, text lessons, quizzes and practice sessions; its homeschool page describes strength scores and revision reminders. Useful for making progress visible and pointing students toward a next step. [Insights for students](https://support.getatomi.com/en/articles/86389-insights-for-students), [Atomi homeschool](https://www.getatomi.com/au/homeschool).

### Education Perfect (educationperfect.com)
- Facts: K-12 learning, assessment and analytics platform. Instruction, practice and assessment in one cycle. Subjects include maths, science, English, languages, humanities, health and PE, DigiTech, EAL. Activity Creator, assessments, NAPLAN, AI student feedback, integrations. 5,000+ schools and 1.8M+ students (their figures). Sold mainly to schools with tailored pricing. Indicative only: one ACT school charged families $84 per student per year for Years 7 to 10 in 2026, and a third-party listing says from $15 per pupil. Its curricula menu lists the Australian Curriculum, Victorian, NSW (including HSC), Cambridge, NZ and Ontario. No WA senior (WACE) option is listed (verify on its curricula page).
- Strong at: school-wide adoption, teacher analytics, evidence and research, security badges, scale.
- Gaps: built for teachers to assign, so weaker for a student working alone at home. No visible WA senior pathway. Drill and lesson style, not interactive simulation.
- We do better: works with no teacher, WA SCSA focus, hands-on scenes, parent-light home use.
- Adopt: the instruction to practice to assessment cycle, individualised next steps after assessment, clear progress dashboards, auto feedback.
- Do not fight on: school contracts, languages, NAPLAN, whole-school analytics.

### Mathspace (mathspace.co/au)
- Facts: adaptive maths only. Step-by-step feedback, hints, diagnostic check-ins, interactive textbooks, Eddie Woo videos. Sold to schools and parents. Pricing not retrieved.
- Strong at: step-level adaptivity in maths.
- Gaps: one subject.
- We do better: nothing worth claiming in maths drill. Our edge would be visual, interactive explainers and a full course, not adaptive practice.
- Adopt: instant step-level feedback and hints on practice questions.
- Do not fight on: adaptive maths practice.

### Reading Eggs (readingeggs.com.au)
- Facts: ages 2 to 13, phonics games, songs, golden-egg rewards, Reading Eggs Junior, Reading Eggspress, Mathseeds. Sold to parents, schools and homeschoolers with a free trial. From 3P Learning. Home pricing not retrieved.
- Strong at: motivation and rewards for young children.
- Gaps: early reading and maths only.
- We do better: nothing in early literacy. Relevant only for how we motivate younger learners later.
- Adopt: simple rewards and progress milestones for younger students.
- Do not fight on: early reading.

### Khan Academy (khanacademy.org)
- Facts: its homepage did not render for our fetch, so details are unverified. Generally known as a free nonprofit with lessons and practice across maths, science and computing, plus an AI tutor. Confirm before quoting.
- Strong at (unverified): free, huge, mastery tracking.
- Gaps (unverified): US-oriented, not mapped to WA SCSA, no complete WA course path.
- We do better: WA alignment, a complete course with project work, hands-on scenes.
- Adopt: mastery and completion tracking, lesson then practice flow.
- Do not fight on: free generic content.
- **Student mastery reference checked 23 Oct 2026:** official help now confirms course-level mastery percentage, unit/skill states (Familiar, Proficient, Mastered), and lesson recommendations after practice or assessment. Useful as a model for showing course and unit progress without hiding the next learning action. [Self-paced Mastery](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360007253831-Using-self-paced-practice-and-Mastery-in-the-classroom).

### Grok Academy (groklearning.com)
- Facts: Australian charity, "your home for Digital Technologies education". Python, HTML, CSS, embedded programming. Problem-based coding in the browser, interactive notes, automarking with feedback. Free competitions (Web.Comp free for Australian students), teaching resources, professional learning, curriculum guidance. ST4S badge. Free to start. Paid details not retrieved. Aimed at teachers and schools.
- Strong at: coding practice with automarking, trusted with Australian schools, closest subject match.
- Gaps: coding focus, not a full WA AIT senior course (client briefs, portfolios, networks and hardware, data representation).
- We do better: broader AIT scope for Y11 and 12, hands-on hardware and network scenes, sequenced course.
- Adopt: problem-based tasks that pair each concept with a challenge, automarked practice, badges and competitions.
- Do not fight on: coding auto-marking. Link to Grok where it fits instead of rebuilding it.
- **Course progression reference checked 23 Oct 2026:** Digital Exploration pairs short topic descriptions with practical activities, including non-coding UX and privacy challenges. Useful for connecting each topic to a concrete action. [Digital Exploration](https://groklearning.com/dex/).

### Code.org / CodeAI (code.org)
- **Student progression reference checked 23 Oct 2026:** official guidance describes a progress view at course/unit, lesson and level granularity, with visible symbols for completed and in-progress work and access to time/last-worked details. Useful for simple, consistent lesson completion states. [Viewing student progress](https://support.code.org/hc/en-us/articles/115000693231-Viewing-student-progress).
- Its curriculum is organized by grade band, unit, lesson and activity. Useful for preserving a clear hierarchy from course overview down to the next activity. [Curriculum](https://code.org/cs/curriculum).

### Education Perfect (educationperfect.com)
- **Student progress reference checked 23 Oct 2026:** the help centre describes individual progress views across lessons, lists, tasks and assessments, including students' answers. Useful for keeping progress attached to actual learning work, not only a course-wide percentage. [View progress reporting](https://help.educationperfect.com/en/articles/14792517-view-progress-reporting).

### Code.org (now CodeAI, code.org)
- Facts: nonprofit, free K-12 curriculum in computer science, AI and data science. AI Foundations (Grades 9 to 12) and AI Discoveries (Grades 6 to 8). Teacher professional development. Reports 150M+ students reached. US-centric.
- Strong at: free, funded, brand, AI literacy content.
- Gaps: not mapped to WA SCSA, teacher-led, US framing.
- We do better: WA alignment, student-led complete course, Y11 and 12 AIT.
- Adopt: game-based activities, AI literacy topics (check relevance to the current syllabus).
- Do not fight on: free K-12 breadth.

## 5. Features worth adopting (seed for the feature backlog, owner to approve before building)

1. Per-student progress and completion view (Atomi, Education Perfect, Khan).
2. Instant feedback on practice and check questions (Mathspace, Education Perfect, Atomi).
3. Revision and exam practice mode for Y11 and 12 (Atomi).
4. Individualised next steps after a check or assessment (Education Perfect).
5. Problem-based tasks with auto-marking where the content allows (Grok).
6. Light rewards and milestones, mainly for younger learners (Reading Eggs).
7. Parent view of progress, useful for home education records (Atomi, Reading Eggs).

## 6. Marketing and claims rules

- Only claim what this doc or the pilot data supports. Mark anything else as a hypothesis.
- Never say a competitor lacks something unless verified on their site that day. Example: do not say Atomi has no WA AIT course until checked.
- Lead with the four advantages in section 1, backed by pilot results once we have them.
- Compare on fit (WA, hands-on, complete course), not by attacking competitors.
